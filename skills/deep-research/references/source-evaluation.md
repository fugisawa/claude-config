# Source Evaluation Guide

## Source Credibility Matrix

### Publisher Tiers

**Tier 1 - Highest Credibility:**
- Peer-reviewed academic journals
- Government agencies and official statistics
- Major wire services (AP, Reuters, AFP)
- Established research institutions
- Official company filings (SEC, regulatory)

**Tier 2 - High Credibility:**
- Major newspapers of record (NYT, WSJ, Guardian, etc.)
- Industry-specific trade publications
- University press releases
- Named expert blogs with credentials
- Professional organization publications

**Tier 3 - Moderate Credibility:**
- General news outlets
- Industry blogs with editorial standards
- Company press releases (for company-specific info)
- Wikipedia (as starting point, verify sources)
- Professional forums with expert participation

**Tier 4 - Use with Caution:**
- Opinion pieces (valuable for perspectives, not facts)
- Social media from verified experts
- Self-published books/articles
- Aggregator sites

**Tier 5 - Low Credibility:**
- Anonymous sources
- Content farms
- Sites with no editorial standards
- Sources with obvious conflicts of interest
- Outdated information without context

## Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Authority Assessment

Questions to ask:
- Who wrote this? What are their credentials?
- What institution published it?
- Is the author a recognized expert?
- Are credentials relevant to the topic?
- Can authorship be verified?

Red flags:
- Anonymous authorship on factual claims
- Credentials unrelated to topic
- No institutional affiliation
- Cannot find author elsewhere

### 2. Accuracy Verification

Questions to ask:
- Are claims supported by evidence?
- Can facts be verified elsewhere?
- Are sources cited?
- Do the citations check out?
- Are statistics contextualized?

Red flags:
- Unsourced claims
- Broken or fake citations
- Statistics without methodology
- Quotes without attribution

### 3. Currency Assessment

Questions to ask:
- When was this published?
- Has the information likely changed?
- Is there more recent information?
- Is historical context appropriate?

Recency requirements by topic type:
- Technology/Software: 6 months - 2 years
- Science/Research: 2-5 years (field dependent)
- Current events: Days to weeks
- Historical: Context matters more than date
- Legal/Regulatory: Check for updates
- Statistics: Note collection date

### 4. Objectivity Analysis

Questions to ask:
- What is the purpose of this content?
- Who funded this research/publication?
- Are multiple perspectives represented?
- Is language neutral or loaded?
- Are limitations acknowledged?

Bias indicators:
- One-sided presentation
- Emotional language
- Missing counterarguments
- Undisclosed funding
- Conflicts of interest

### 5. Coverage Depth

Questions to ask:
- Is the topic thoroughly explored?
- Are nuances addressed?
- Is context provided?
- Are limitations discussed?
- Does it address obvious questions?

## Cross-Verification Protocol

### The Triangle Rule
For any critical claim, seek three independent sources:
1. Primary source (original study, official document, direct statement)
2. Analysis source (expert interpretation, news coverage)
3. Alternative perspective (different viewpoint or critique)

### Independence Verification
Sources are truly independent when:
- Different organizations/publishers
- Different geographic origins (when relevant)
- Different methodologies
- Not citing each other as primary source

### Disagreement Resolution
When sources conflict:
1. Check publication dates - is one outdated?
2. Check methodology - are they measuring the same thing?
3. Check scope - are they addressing the same question?
4. Check for corrections or retractions
5. Weight by source credibility tier
6. Acknowledge the disagreement in findings

## Domain-Specific Evaluation

### Scientific/Medical Claims
- Peer-review status is essential
- Check for replication studies
- Note study size and methodology
- Distinguish preliminary from established findings
- Check for consensus statements from professional bodies

### Legal/Regulatory Information
- Jurisdiction matters
- Check for recent changes
- Prefer official government sources
- Note effective dates
- Distinguish between law and interpretation

### Financial/Business Information
- SEC filings for public companies
- Note self-reported vs. audited data
- Check for conflicts in analyst reports
- Distinguish projections from results
- Verify with multiple financial news sources

### Technology Information
- Documentation version must match
- Community consensus on best practices
- Distinguish official from community content
- Check for deprecation notices
- Prefer recent for fast-changing tech

## Quick Reference Checklist

For each key source, verify:
- [ ] Author/publisher identified and credible
- [ ] Publication date appropriate for topic
- [ ] Claims supported by evidence
- [ ] No obvious conflicts of interest
- [ ] Information corroborated elsewhere
- [ ] Limitations acknowledged
- [ ] Still current/not superseded
